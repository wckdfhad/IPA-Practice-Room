IPA Practice Room

IPA Practice Room /ˈpræktɪs/

Tap a symbol to hear the sound on its own, then test yourself. You can also switch to an example word, with the letters that make the sound underlined.

This browser cannot speak example words aloud. The sound-only mode still works.

The sound-only clips are built from real human recordings taken from the audio on Wikipedia's IPA charts. Stops and glides (/p b t d k g tʃ dʒ r j w/) cannot be said on their own, so they are played with a short "a" after them. The same goes for /θ/, which is very faint on its own. /θ/ and /ð/ have three versions each: tap Other version in the sound chart to hear the next one, and the quizzes use the version you last chose. Hissing and humming sounds (/s ʃ f z m n/ and similar) are held steady so you can hear them clearly. Each diphthong is a smooth glide from one recorded vowel to the next. For /ʌ/ the recording is the near-open central vowel, which is close to the British sound, and the recordings do not show the length difference between short and long vowels. Use the example word to hear natural speech. The word voice menu lists the English voices installed on your device.
